## Post-specific image direction

- First decide whether an image genuinely adds context or visual meaning. Text-only is often stronger than a decorative image. A still image is usually sufficient for a single explanatory founder post; do not default to video.
- Translate the actual post into a visual proposition before writing a prompt. For savings circles, make pooled contributions and one-at-a-time payout legible. A group arranged around a shared reservoir with a single highlighted return path is clearer than a generic left-to-right payments diagram.
- The study in `11_03_03 PM.png` is the stronger visual fit for the savings-circle post because it makes a shared central pool, group participation and one active payout readable together. The `11_07_44 PM.png` study has a more abstract, linear input/output flow and more negative space, but reads less clearly as a rotating community savings circle. Both use Sluice's palette and controlled amber flow. Treat this as a comparison for this post, not a permanent ranking for all future assets.
- Review generated counts and relationships. The copy's “twenty people” is an example, so the image can use abstract equal markers rather than inaccurately implying a precise count. If it depicts a precise count, verify it. Do not let generation errors change the product mechanic.
- Image styles may be specific, vague, abstract, diagrammatic, photographic, or motion-led depending on what best serves the post. Don't force every topic into the reservoir diagram.

## Quality bar and exclusions

Aim for premium, modern, ownable, legible at mobile size, and coherent with the Sluice references. Favor an intentional visual system—consistent geometry, palette, lighting, hierarchy, and motif behavior—over a pile of fashionable effects. Web3 does not have one universal visual style. Current brand examples such as Base's flexible brand system and Aptos's deliberate move beyond dark/neon crypto conventions are useful reminders to build a coherent identity rather than imitate category clichés; they are not visual references to copy.

Avoid defaulting to neon gradients, glowing blockchain cubes, chains, coins, token symbols, trading dashboards, random network nodes, glassmorphism, generic sci-fi interfaces, or excessive lens flares. Don't generate documentary-style West African people and imply they depict a real group. For real people or locations, prefer authentic, properly licensed imagery with appropriate consent and context.

Never ask an image model to redraw or recreate the Sluice mark. Keep the mark and typography out of generated artwork when exactness matters; overlay approved original assets afterward. Pinterest can be used to find visual references, not as proof that an image is licensed. Verify the source and rights before reusing sourced images.

## Prompt or production workflow

When writing an image prompt, specify:

1. The post's exact idea and what a viewer should understand at a glance.
2. A tailored visual metaphor and the important spatial relationships (what pools, what moves, what is highlighted).
3. Sluice palette, geometry, material, lighting, balance, and negative-space guidance drawn from current assets.
4. Crop/aspect ratio and a mobile-legibility requirement appropriate to the intended platform.
5. Exclusions that prevent likely clichés, inaccurate mechanics, fabricated text/logos, and culturally misleading imagery.

Attach the current Sluice symbol/banner as brand references only when the image tool supports them. Explicitly tell the generator to borrow palette and atmosphere, not copy their composition or reconstruct the logo. Prefer a clean image without words; add exact copy and the approved mark afterward in a design tool. Deliver prompts that are self-contained and ready to paste into the named generator. For direct image creation, use the available image-generation capability when appropriate and visually inspect the result before handing it over.

For current platform specs, licensing, or a request for premium/current design research, verify current primary/official sources and cite concise findings outside any post-ready copy. Treat “2026 style” as a request for current, intentional craft—not as permission to claim a universal trend or apply a trend merely because it is new.
